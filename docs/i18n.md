# Languages

English is the site. Other languages are a layer the browser chooses — no server, no second site.
Decided 2026-10-28 (hybrid, Czech first).

## What is translated, and what is not

- **Ours, reviewed:** the shared bar's menus, the front page, `/situations/` and the site footer.
  This is where a wrong word is a wrong legal claim, so these texts are written by us against a
  fixed glossary (`app/src/site/glossary.ts`) and approved by a native speaker — a lawyer for the
  legal parts — before a language ships.
- **The browser's:** every service screen (xNotary, xSignature, xConvert) stays English. A reader
  may let the browser translate it. In a non-English language the bar says so, and that a browser
  translator may send the page's text to its maker; the documents themselves never are.

## How a language is chosen

`app/src/site/lang.svelte.ts`: `?lang=cs`, then the reader's last choice (localStorage
`site.lang`, this browser only), then — for a *reviewed* language only — the browser's preferred
language. English otherwise. The switcher in the bar offers every language to every visitor
(owner's decision, 2026-10-28).

## The review gate

`LANGUAGES.cs.reviewed` is `false` until someone signs Czech off. Until then Czech is never chosen
automatically — a Czech browser starts in English — though anyone can pick it in the switcher.
To review:

    cd app && npm run -s i18n:review > review-cs.md

lists every Czech text beside its English source, with the glossary. Read the short interface
texts on the page itself at `/?lang=cs` and `/situations/?lang=cs`. When approved, set `reviewed:
true` — and record who approved it and when, in the commit.

## Rules for Czech

- The statutory term, in any inflection, wherever the English uses the legal term
  (`GLOSSARY_CS`): *zaručený* (advanced), *uznávaný* (recognised), *kvalifikovaný*, *úředně
  ověřený podpis*, *registr obyvatel*, *veřejná listina*, *notářský zápis*.
- Never a near miss (`FORBIDDEN_CS`): *pokročilý podpis*, *uznaný podpis*, *notarizace*, or any
  claim that the site verifies, certifies or legalises.
- Every limit the English states is stated in Czech too; `situations.test.ts` checks the ones that
  matter most.

## Adding a language

Add it to `LANGUAGES` (unreviewed), a `situations.<lang>.ts` mirroring `situations.cs.ts`, a branch
in `situationsIn`, the `TEXT` entries in the four components, the `<lang>` fields in `services.ts`,
and a glossary for it with the same tests. Keep `reviewed: false` until it is approved.

## Browser translation: what it breaks, and the guards

Chrome's and Edge's page translators replace text nodes with their own. Two consequences, both
found by emulating that rewriting in a headless browser (the check lives in the session
scratchpad as `translate-sim.mjs`; rerun it after large UI changes):

1. **Stale text.** Svelte updates a text in the node it created, which the translator has taken
   off the page; the old text stays on screen. Guards: `/situations/` rebuilds its panel per step
   and its stepper per journey (`{#key}`); every button whose label says what the click will do
   renders it through `app/src/site/Fresh.svelte`.
2. **Data translated, or sent.** Names from certificates, signer and issuer names, file names,
   hashes, URLs and product names carry `translate="no"`. Keep adding it to anything that shows
   such data.
